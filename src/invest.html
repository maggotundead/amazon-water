Water Extraction Rights

Water Extraction Rights in Perpetuity: Karst Aquifer, Part of the Huge South American Guarani Aquifer System

It is very important for investors to understand the extent of the water resource at ANSW’s fingertips. The Karst Aquifer in the Metropolitan Area of Curitiba-Brazil is part of the Guarani Aquifer System, the second-largest known aquifer system in the world and a significant source of fresh, drinking water.

The aquifer extends beneath Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay, covering approximately 1.2 million square kilometres and holding an estimated volume of around 40,000 cubic kilometres. Its depth ranges from approximately 50 metres to 1,800 metres below the surface and it is estimated to contain around 37,000 cubic kilometres of fresh water.

The aquifer’s recharge rate is around 0.5% per annum from precipitation, so it is a delicately balanced ecosystem and drawing water from the aquifer is thus highly regulated by the governing countries. For example, it has taken ANSW almost 20 years of perseverance to attain its exclusive extraction rights over a 49-hectare area located over a ‘Karst’ (limestone) aquifer, and this timescale is typical for such licences to be approved; clearly such a timescale now acts very much in ANSW’s favour with respect to other potentially competing water bottling companies who seek to tap this resource.

Premium Water Quality

“Excellent” Quality Water, Neutral PH: The water emanating from the karst aquifer is of very high quality as confirmed by a number of professional bodies. Firstly, Zenith International (zenithglobal.com) which as a well-known and 30 years established food & drinks consultancy and very active in the bottled water market, undertook a Water Sampling and Analysis Technical Review, later updated in 2022 by a local Brazilian mining engineering company, Terra Engineering. Zenith’s report describes the water as “excellent” quality. The water’s PH is around neutral, i.e., around 7.0 and it contains no chemical or organic contaminants or any metallic elements. Further, a leading UK-based specialist – Professor Richard Taylor of University College, London – who is an authority on water quality analysis has described ANSW’s water extracted from the Karst Aquifer as an “extremely high quality” Calcium Bicarbonate water.
